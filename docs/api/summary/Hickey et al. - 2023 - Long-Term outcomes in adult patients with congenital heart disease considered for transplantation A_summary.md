**Core Objective:** To identify hemodynamic, functional, and laboratory parameters that predict 1‑year and 10‑year survival in adult congenital heart disease (ACHD) patients evaluated for heart or multiorgan transplantation using a machine‑learning decision‑tree model.

**Patient Cohort & Study Scope:** 58 ACHD patients (mean age 35.8 years, range 29–44), 65.5% male, evaluated between November 2004 and July 2014 at a single UPMC center; all were considered for heart or heart‑plus‑additional‑organ transplant.

**AI/ML Approach:** Supervised decision‑tree analysis (JMP) incorporating all significant univariate variables (e.g., invasive hemodynamics, CPET, number of sternotomies). Input data were tabular clinical variables from electronic medical records; no imaging or deep‑learning models were used.

**Key Findings:**  
- The decision tree identified pre‑listing systemic venous pressure (SVP) < 14 mmHg as the strongest predictor of 1‑year survival, yielding **100% survival** in transplanted patients (no AUC/accuracy reported).  
- For non‑transplanted patients, pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (PCWP) < 15 mmHg also correlated with **100% 1‑year survival**.  
- Transplantation conferred a 5.15‑fold increase in 1‑year survival and a 5.44‑fold increase in 10‑year survival versus waitlisted patients.  
- SVP ≥ 14 mmHg reduced 1‑year survival to 66.7%; PCWP ≥ 17 mmHg predicted 0% 10‑year survival in waitlisted/declined patients.  
- No conventional performance metrics (AUC, C‑statistic, accuracy) were reported.

**Main Limitations:** Retrospective single‑center design with a small, heterogeneous sample (n = 58) limits generalizability and external validation.
