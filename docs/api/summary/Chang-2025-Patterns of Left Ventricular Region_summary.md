**Core Objective:** To identify and characterize distinct regional wall motion abnormality (RWMA) phenotypes in brain‑dead heart donors using a machine learning algorithm, and to assess their association with donor heart acceptance and recipient 2‑year survival.  

**Patient Cohort & Study Scope:** Analytical cohort of 913 adult heart donors (age mean 34.3 ± 11.7 y) with any RWMA, derived from the Donor Heart Study (4,333 potential donors enrolled 2018‑2023). All donors were brain‑dead, ages 18‑65, and underwent transthoracic echocardiography.  

**AI/ML Approach:** Unsupervised k‑means clustering applied to the 17 segmental Wall Motion Scores (WMS) from initial TTEs; clusters were consolidated into four clinically plausible RWMA phenotypes (focal basal septal, basal + midventricular, apical, global hypokinesis).  

**Key Findings:**  
- Four RWMA phenotypes identified: focal basal septal (n = 500, 55%), basal/midventricular (n = 311, 34%), apical (n = 66, 7%), global hypokinesis (n = 36, 4%).  
- Initial LVEF differed markedly (FBS 54.3% ± 9.1 vs. global hypokinesis 30.1% ± 10.2; SMD = 1.185).  
- Troponin‑I and NT‑proBNP were lowest in FBS (e.g., NT‑proBNP 2,609 pg/mL) and highest in basal/midventricular (7,937 pg/mL).  
- Donor heart acceptance highest for FBS phenotype (68% of hearts accepted).  
- Recipient 2‑year survival showed no significant differences across phenotypes (overall ≈ 84%; P = 0.67).  
- All phenotypes demonstrated significant improvement in LVEF and WMS index on repeat TTE (n = 314).  

**Main Limitations:** Retrospective, single‑center clustering with subjective consolidation of k‑means clusters and exclusion of 70 outliers (<10% of sample).
